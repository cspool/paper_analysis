# VIII. RELATED WORK

*1) Computation-Communication Overlapping:* Overlapping communication with computation is a widely studied method to address communication challenges in distributed AI systems, explored in various parallelism schemes [6], [17], [21], [51], [62]. CoCoNet [21] first introduces kernel fusion to overlap AllReduce with GEMM operations. Centauri [6] employs multi-dimensional communication partitioning and hierarchical dataflow-graph scheduling to promote overlap in hybrid parallelism. T3 [51] leverages hardware primitives for overlap in tensor parallelism. However, these approaches are primarily designed for regular large-model workloads and do not address the challenges introduced by the inherent dynamism of MoE models.

*2) MoE-Specific Optimizations:* Early MoE systems enforced fixed token capacities per expert to simplify compilation and execution [31], [53], though this often came at the cost of model accuracy [12], [13]. Later systems introduced CPUassisted dynamic shape support, which enabled a range of optimizations to study dynamic communication bottlenecks in MoE, including improving the implementation of Allto-All operations [19], [58], [68], [69] and exploring compute–communication overlap strategies at multiple granularities, ranging from graph-level overlap (e.g., FasterMoE [16], Tutel [19]) to finer-grained overlap (e.g., Comet [67], CC-Fuser [61]). Recent work has identified the significant overhead of CPU coordination and attempts to build fully GPUdriven computation-communication pipelines, such as via GPU-side address synchronization (e.g., Primus-Turbo [3]) or kernel fusion (e.g., FlashDMoE [2]). Nevertheless, the overlap mechanisms proposed by these works often impose trade-offs between performance and programmability. In contrast, MoE-Hub removes address-resolution-induced synchronization from the critical path and uses hardware-managed dataflow orchestration to achieve efficient fine-grained overlap without burdening the software stack.
