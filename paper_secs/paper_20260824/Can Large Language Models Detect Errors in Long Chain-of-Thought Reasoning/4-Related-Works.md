# 4 Related Works

Test-time Scaling. Recently, many works have begun to explore the test-time scaling techniques [\[Ope](#page-15-3)[nAI,](#page-15-3) [2024,](#page-15-3) [Snell et al.,](#page-18-9) [2024\]](#page-18-9), can greatly enhance performance by increasing the number of generated tokens. Several methods [\[Guan et al.,](#page-18-10) [2025,](#page-18-10) [Chen et al.,](#page-18-11) [2024b,](#page-18-11) [Hao et al.,](#page-18-12) [2023,](#page-18-12) [Yao et al.,](#page-19-0) [2023\]](#page-19-0) have developed the tree search methods to improve reasoning capabilities. In addition, o1-like models (e.g., o1, R1) [\[OpenAI,](#page-15-3) [2024,](#page-15-3) [Guo et al.,](#page-15-4) [2025,](#page-15-4) [Team et al.,](#page-19-1) [2025\]](#page-19-1) have investigated the reinforcement learning methods to generate long CoTs and enhance the model performance. Some methods have also begun exploring the intrinsic mechanisms of generating long CoTs [\[Yeo et al.,](#page-15-6) [2025,](#page-15-6) [Wu et al.,](#page-19-2) [2024a\]](#page-19-2).

Process Reward Modeling. PRMs demonstrate a significant advantage over traditional outcomelevel reward models (ORMs) in enhancing the accuracy of process reasoning [\[Lightman et al.,](#page-18-1) [2023\]](#page-18-1). The development of an increasing number of PRMs [\[Zhang et al.,](#page-19-3) [2025a,](#page-19-3) [Xia et al.,](#page-19-4) [2024,](#page-19-4) [o1 Team,](#page-19-5) [2024\]](#page-19-5) offers valuable contributions to multi-step reasoning. In addition, the introduction of numerous human-annotated process-level datasets [\[Cobbe et al.,](#page-16-6) [2021,](#page-16-6) [Wu et al.,](#page-19-6) [2023,](#page-19-6) [Li et al.,](#page-19-7) [2024b\]](#page-19-7) provides essential resources for research. These advancements have collectively spurred exploration in the research direction of generating long CoT.

LLM Critic. The critique capabilities of LLMs have drawn great interests [\[Zhang et al.,](#page-19-8) [2025b,](#page-19-8) [Liu](#page-19-9) [et al.,](#page-19-9) [2025\]](#page-19-9). For example, CriticBench [\[Luo et al.,](#page-19-10) [2023,](#page-19-10) [Zheng et al.,](#page-20-0) [2024b\]](#page-20-0) uses LLMs to generate critiques and binary verdicts for input solutions, measuring accuracy by comparing these verdicts to ground truth labels. CriticEval [\[Lan et al.,](#page-15-7) [2024\]](#page-15-7) evaluates both feedback and correction qualities. Additionally, many works have explored the LLMs' self-critique for improving reasoning [\[Tyen et al.,](#page-20-1) [2023,](#page-20-1) [Stechly et al.,](#page-20-2) [2024\]](#page-20-2). For example, [Huang et al.](#page-20-3) [\[2023\]](#page-20-3) have highlighted the challenges in self-correction without external feedback. ProCo [\[Wu et al.,](#page-20-4) [2024b\]](#page-20-4) facilitates self-correction and iterative verification processes for better critical abilities.
