# <span id="page-9-0"></span>**6 Related Work**

**Improving long-context modeling.** Language models' context sizes have been significantly expanded thanks to recent advances in pre-training and post-training methods [\(Gao](#page-11-4) [et al.,](#page-11-4) [2024;](#page-11-4) [Fu et al.,](#page-11-3) [2024;](#page-11-3) [Xiong et al.,](#page-14-8) [2023;](#page-14-8) [An et al.,](#page-10-4) [2024;](#page-10-4) [Hu et al.,](#page-12-8) [2024\)](#page-12-8), alternative attention mechanisms [\(Xiao et al.,](#page-14-9) [2023;](#page-14-9) [Bertsch et al.,](#page-10-5) [2023;](#page-10-5) [Jin et al.,](#page-12-9) [2024;](#page-12-9) [Yen et al.,](#page-15-6) [2024\)](#page-15-6) or architectures [\(Gu & Dao,](#page-11-2) [2024;](#page-11-2) [Lieber et al.,](#page-13-8) [2024;](#page-13-8) [Peng et al.,](#page-13-9) [2023\)](#page-13-9), context compression approaches [\(Xiao et al.,](#page-14-9) [2023;](#page-14-9) [Xu et al.,](#page-15-7) [2024a;](#page-15-7) [Zhang et al.,](#page-15-8) [2023\)](#page-15-8), and position extrapolation techniques [\(Chen et al.,](#page-10-6) [2024;](#page-10-6) [2023;](#page-10-7) [Ding et al.,](#page-11-12) [2024;](#page-11-12) [Peng et al.,](#page-13-10) [2024;](#page-13-10) [Zhu et al.,](#page-15-9) [2024\)](#page-15-9). While we primarily focus on evaluating different model families, our dataset can also be used to assess the effectiveness of position extrapolation or context compression techniques.

**Evaluating long-context models.** In [§2,](#page-2-1) we have discussed the limitations of multiple commonly used existing LCLMs benchmarks. In addition to those discussed in [§2,](#page-2-1) some application-centric benchmarks focus on specific domains [\(Wang et al.,](#page-14-4) [2024b;](#page-14-4) [Karpinska](#page-12-4) [et al.,](#page-12-4) [2024;](#page-12-4) [Chang et al.,](#page-10-8) [2024;](#page-10-8) [Kim et al.,](#page-12-10) [2024;](#page-12-10) [Wang et al.,](#page-14-10) [2024a;](#page-14-10) [Li et al.,](#page-12-11) [2024b;](#page-12-11) [Bertsch](#page-10-9) [et al.,](#page-10-9) [2024;](#page-10-9) [Lee et al.,](#page-12-12) [2024\)](#page-12-12). In contrast, LONGPROC focuses on general long-form generation, covering a diverse set of tasks while requiring significantly longer generation lengths.

**Executing procedures with models.** There also exists prior work that studies models' performance on specific operations like addition to investigate their length generation capabilities [\(Nye et al.,](#page-13-11) [2021;](#page-13-11) [Dziri et al.,](#page-11-13) [2023;](#page-11-13) [Zhou et al.,](#page-15-10) [2024\)](#page-15-10) or algorithmic reasoning capabilities [\(Gu et al.,](#page-11-14) [2024;](#page-11-14) [Wang et al.,](#page-14-11) [2023;](#page-14-11) [Gandhi et al.,](#page-11-9) [2024;](#page-11-9) [Valmeekam et al.,](#page-14-12) [2023;](#page-14-12) [Sel et al.,](#page-13-12) [2024;](#page-13-12) [Borazjanizadeh et al.,](#page-10-10) [2024\)](#page-10-10). Our benchmark features a broader range of procedures beyond algorithmic ones and emphasizes extended length to test long-form generation capabilities. In particular, DOLOMITES [\(Malaviya et al.,](#page-13-7) [2024\)](#page-13-7) evaluates models on methodical writing tasks given general procedural steps (high-level plans as opposed to detailed procedures). However, its inputs and outputs are short by the standard of LCLMs and its solutions are open-ended solutions.
