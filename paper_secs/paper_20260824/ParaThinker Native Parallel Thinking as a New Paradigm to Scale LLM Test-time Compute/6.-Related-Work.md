# **6. Related Work**

#### **6.1. Sequential Test-Time Scaling**

Recent advances in test-time scaling seek to improve LLM reasoning by increasing computational depth during decoding, primarily through reinforcement learning (RL) [\(OpenAI,](#page-17-0) [2024;](#page-17-0) [DeepSeek-AI,](#page-16-0) [2025;](#page-16-0) [Google,](#page-16-6) [2025;](#page-16-6) [Yang et al.,](#page-19-0) [2025a;](#page-19-0) [Team et al.,](#page-18-0) [2025;](#page-18-0) [Zhao et al.,](#page-19-4) [2025;](#page-19-4) [Yu et al.,](#page-19-5) [2025\)](#page-19-5) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) [\(Muennighoff et al.,](#page-17-5) [2025;](#page-17-5) [Ye et al.,](#page-19-2) [2025\)](#page-19-2). RL-based methods [\(OpenAI,](#page-17-0) [2024;](#page-17-0) [Shao](#page-18-6) [et al.,](#page-18-6) [2024\)](#page-18-6) encourage LLMs to allocate more computation to promising reasoning paths by encouraging self-reflection and iterative trial. Other approaches distill long-form rationales from larger teacher models into smaller student models, enabling deeper internal reasoning through fine-tuned CoT supervision [\(Labs,](#page-17-7) [2025;](#page-17-7) [Ye et al.,](#page-19-2) [2025;](#page-19-2) [Xu et al.,](#page-19-6) [2025;](#page-19-6) [Geiping et al.,](#page-16-7) [2025\)](#page-16-7). While these methods significantly enhance LLM performance on complex tasks, they often suffer from increased inference latency and compute consumption due to long output sequences [\(Sun et al.,](#page-18-3) [2025;](#page-18-3) [Zhu & Li,](#page-19-7) [2025;](#page-19-7) [Qu et al.,](#page-17-8) [2025;](#page-17-8) [Wen et al.,](#page-18-7) [2025\)](#page-18-7). Moreover, excessively long reasoning traces may introduce "overthinking" effects such as repetition or hallucination [\(Chen et al.,](#page-15-0) [2025b;](#page-15-0) [Ghosal et al.,](#page-16-1) [2025\)](#page-16-1). Besides, recent works have also shown that sequential reasoning LLMs are brittle to reasoning order [\(Chen et al.,](#page-16-8) [2024b\)](#page-16-8) or shallow token attacks [\(Xu et al.,](#page-19-8) [2024;](#page-19-8) [Qi et al.,](#page-17-9) [2025\)](#page-17-9). In contrast, our method introduces a new dimension of inference-time scaling—width—by executing multiple reasoning paths in parallel and summarizing them. This approach preserves reasoning efficiency while avoiding long single-path decoding.

## **6.2. Search-Based Methods for Parallel Reasoning**

Parallel Decoding LLMs improve reasoning by sampling multiple tokens at each step to accelerate LLM inference and/or improving LLM performance. Early techniques include beam search [\(Wiseman & Rush,](#page-19-9) [2016\)](#page-19-9), self-consistency [\(Wang et al.,](#page-18-8) [2023\)](#page-18-8), speculative decoding [\(Leviathan et al.,](#page-17-10) [2023;](#page-17-10) [Chen et al.,](#page-15-3) [2023\)](#page-15-3) and majority voting [\(Chen et al.,](#page-15-1) [2024a\)](#page-15-1). Recent advancements include Best-of-N [\(Lightman et al.,](#page-17-11) [2023\)](#page-17-11), Tree of Thoughts (ToT) [\(Yao et al.,](#page-19-10) [2023\)](#page-19-10), and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) [\(Snell et al.,](#page-18-1) [2024;](#page-18-1) [Guan et al.,](#page-16-9) [2025\)](#page-16-9). These approaches typically require an external verifier to evaluate and rank candidate completions, increasing computational cost and often relying on domain-specific or manually constructed reward signals. Our method departs from these paradigms by generating multiple reasoning trajectories internally and merging them using a lightweight summarization step, without requiring external verifiers or retraining.
