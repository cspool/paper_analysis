# 2 Related Work

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. We use the term chain-of-thought broadly to refer to methods that generate an intermediate reasoning process in language before outputting the final answer. This includes prompting LLMs [\(Wei et al.,](#page-13-0) [2022;](#page-13-0) [Khot et al.,](#page-12-6) [2022;](#page-12-6) [Zhou et al.,](#page-13-3) [2022\)](#page-13-3), or training LLMs to generate reasoning chains, either with supervised finetuning [\(Yue et al.,](#page-13-4) [2023;](#page-13-4) [Yu et al.,](#page-13-5) [2023\)](#page-13-5) or reinforcement learning [\(Wang et al.,](#page-13-6) [2024;](#page-13-6) [Havrilla et al.,](#page-12-7) [2024;](#page-12-7) [Shao et al.,](#page-12-8) [2024;](#page-12-8) [Yu et al.,](#page-13-7) [2024a\)](#page-13-7). [Madaan and Yazdanbakhsh](#page-12-3) [\(2022\)](#page-12-3) classified the tokens in CoT into symbols, patterns, and text, and proposed to guide the LLM to generate concise CoT based on analysis of their roles. Recent theoretical analyses have demonstrated the usefulness of CoT from the perspective of model expressivity [\(Feng et al.,](#page-11-7) [2023;](#page-11-7) [Merrill and Sabharwal,](#page-12-9) [2023;](#page-12-9) [Li et al.,](#page-12-10) [2024\)](#page-12-10). By employing CoT, the effective depth of the transformer increases because the generated outputs are looped back to the input [\(Feng et al.,](#page-11-7) [2023\)](#page-11-7). These analyses, combined with the established effectiveness of CoT, motivated our design of feeding the continuous thoughts back into the LLM as input embeddings. While CoT has proven effective for certain tasks, its autoregressive generation nature makes it challenging to mimic human reasoning on more complex problems [\(LeCun,](#page-12-11) [2022;](#page-12-11) [Hao et al.,](#page-12-4) [2023\)](#page-12-4), which typically require planning and search. There are works that equip LLMs with explicit tree search algorithms [\(Xie et al.,](#page-13-8) [2023;](#page-13-8) [Yao et al.,](#page-13-2) [2023;](#page-13-2) [Hao et al.,](#page-12-12) [2024\)](#page-12-12), or train the LLM on search dynamics and trajectories [\(Lehnert et al.,](#page-12-13) [2024;](#page-12-13) [Gandhi](#page-11-8) [et al.,](#page-11-8) [2024;](#page-11-8) [Su et al.,](#page-12-14) [2024\)](#page-12-14). In our analysis, we find that after removing the constraint of a language space, a new reasoning pattern similar to BFS emerges, even though the model is not explicitly trained in this way.

Latent reasoning in LLMs. Previous works mostly define latent reasoning in LLMs as the hidden computation in transformers [\(Yang et al.,](#page-13-9) [2024;](#page-13-9) [Biran et al.,](#page-11-9) [2024\)](#page-11-9). [Yang et al.](#page-13-9) [\(2024\)](#page-13-9) constructed a dataset of two-hop reasoning problems and discovered that it is possible to recover the intermediate variable from the hidden representations. [Biran et al.](#page-11-9) [\(2024\)](#page-11-9) further proposed to intervene the latent reasoning by "back-patching" the hidden representation. [Shalev et al.](#page-12-15) [\(2024\)](#page-12-15) discovered parallel latent reasoning paths in LLMs. Another line of work has discovered that, even if the model generates a CoT to reason, the model may actually utilize a different latent reasoning process. This phenomenon is known as the unfaithfulness of CoT reasoning [\(Wang](#page-13-10) [et al.,](#page-13-10) [2022;](#page-13-10) [Turpin et al.,](#page-12-16) [2024\)](#page-12-16). To enhance the latent reasoning of LLMs, previous research proposed to augment it with additional tokens. [Goyal et al.](#page-11-10) [\(2023\)](#page-11-10) pretrained the model by randomly inserting a learnable <pause> tokens to the training corpus. This improves LLM's performance on a variety of tasks, especially when followed by supervised finetuning with <pause> tokens. On the other hand, [Pfau et al.](#page-12-17) [\(2024\)](#page-12-17) further explored the usage of filler tokens, e.g., "...", and concluded that they work well for highly parallelizable problems. However, [Pfau et al.](#page-12-17) [\(2024\)](#page-12-17) mentioned these methods do not extend the expressivity of the LLM like CoT; hence, they may not scale to more general and complex reasoning problems. [Wang](#page-13-11) [et al.](#page-13-11) [\(2023\)](#page-13-11) proposed to predict a planning token as a discrete latent variable before generating the next reasoning step. Recently, it has also been found that one can "internalize" the CoT reasoning into latent reasoning in the transformer with knowledge distillation [\(Deng et al.,](#page-11-11) [2023\)](#page-11-11) or a special training curriculum which gradually shortens CoT [\(Deng et al.,](#page-11-5) [2024\)](#page-11-5). [Yu et al.](#page-13-12) [\(2024b\)](#page-13-12) also proposed to distill a model that can reason latently from data generated with complex reasoning algorithms. These training methods can be combined to our framework, and specifically, we find that breaking down the learning of continuous thoughts into multiple stages, inspired by iCoT [\(Deng et al.,](#page-11-5) [2024\)](#page-11-5), is very beneficial for the training. Other work explores alternative architectures for latent reasoning, including looped transformers [\(Giannou et al.,](#page-11-12) [2023;](#page-11-12) [Fan et al.,](#page-11-13) [2024\)](#page-11-13), diffusion models in sentence embedding space [\(Barrault et al.,](#page-11-14) [2024\)](#page-11-14). Different from these works, we focus on general multi-step reasoning tasks and aim to investigate the unique properties of latent reasoning in comparison to language space. In addition to reasoning tasks, [Pham et al.](#page-12-18) [\(2023\)](#page-12-18) also explored using continuous space for multi-agent communication. Building on Coconut, [Zhu et al.](#page-13-13) [\(2025b\)](#page-13-13) developed a theoretical framework demonstrating that continuous CoT can be more efficient than discrete CoT on certain tasks by encoding multiple reasoning paths in superposition states. Subsequently, [Zhu et al.](#page-13-14) [\(2025a\)](#page-13-14) analyzed the training dynamics to explain how such superposition emerges under the Coconut training objective.
