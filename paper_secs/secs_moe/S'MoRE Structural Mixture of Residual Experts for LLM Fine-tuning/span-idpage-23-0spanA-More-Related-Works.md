# <span id="page-23-0"></span>A More Related Works

More related works on MoE. In addition to the works mentioned in [§2,](#page-1-0) we list a few additional works that explore MoE designs in LLM. LLaMA-MoE [\[Zhu et al., 2024\]](#page-15-6) applies Mixture-of-Experts under the Continual Pre-training (CPT) setting. It breaks down LLaMA's pre-trained weight matrices into sub-matrices and use them to initialize the experts' parameters. MC-SMoE [\[Li et al., 2024b\]](#page-12-11) discovers that there exists high redundancy among experts, and correspondingly proposes algorithms to cluster, merge and then compress a model with many experts. MoA [\[Feng et al., 2024\]](#page-12-12) explores Mixture-of-LoRAs for the multi-task tuning scenarios, where it trains a LoRA for each task separately, and then assembles the multiple LoRAs into an MoE where a learnable router selects the most suitable expert based on the task category.

<span id="page-23-1"></span>Scaling behavior. There is an emerging trend to research the unique scaling behaviors of MoE systems compared with dense models. [Ludziejewski et al.](#page-13-3) [\[2024\]](#page-13-3) summarizes a scaling law indicating that finer expert granularity may improve model capacity, and [He](#page-12-6) [\[2024\]](#page-12-6) provides a practical implementation for very large number of experts. [Zhao et al.](#page-15-2) [\[2025\]](#page-15-2) applies such fine-grained experts in the fine-tuning tasks. Through analysis and experiments, we hypothesize that "structural flexibility" may be a neglected yet critical factor impacting MoE scaling. S'MoRE has the potential to scale better than regular MoEs, for fine-tuning tasks and beyond. We leave the application of S'MoRE on other types of tasks as future work.
